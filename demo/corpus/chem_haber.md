# Chemistry: The Haber Process

## What is the Haber process
The Haber process is the industrial method for making ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen. Ammonia is the main ingredient of nitrogen fertilisers, so the process feeds roughly half of the world's population.

## The reaction
The reaction is N2 + 3H2 <-> 2NH3. It is reversible and exothermic, which means it releases 92 kJ of heat per mole of nitrogen converted. Because it is reversible, the mixture never converts completely.

## Steps in the plant
First, nitrogen is taken from the air and hydrogen is made from natural gas. Second, the gases are mixed and compressed to about 200 atmospheres. Third, the mixture passes over an iron catalyst at 450 degrees Celsius. Fourth, the hot gases are cooled so that ammonia liquefies and is removed. Finally, unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled back to the reactor.

## Choosing conditions
A high pressure favours ammonia because there are fewer gas molecules on the right-hand side. A low temperature favours the exothermic forward reaction, but it makes the reaction too slow. The 450 degree compromise gives a yield of about 15 percent per pass, and recycling raises the overall conversion above 95 percent.

## Effect of changing conditions
Increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium towards ammonia and increases yield. Increasing temperature shifts it towards the reactants and reduces yield. Adding a catalyst does not change the yield, but it makes equilibrium arrive faster.

## Key terms
Equilibrium is the state where forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate. A catalyst is a substance that speeds up a reaction without being used up. Le Chatelier's principle states that a system at equilibrium shifts to oppose any change imposed on it.

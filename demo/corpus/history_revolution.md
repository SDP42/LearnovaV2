# The Industrial Revolution

## Overview
The Industrial Revolution was the shift from hand production to machine manufacturing that began in Britain around 1760 and spread across Europe and North America during the 19th century. It changed where people lived, how they worked and how goods were made.

## Timeline of key events
In 1764 James Hargreaves invented the spinning jenny, which let one worker spin several threads at once. In 1769 James Watt patented an improved steam engine. In 1807 Robert Fulton launched the first commercial steamboat. In 1825 the Stockton and Darlington Railway opened as the first public steam railway. In 1856 Henry Bessemer introduced a cheap process for making steel.

## Causes
Several factors came together. Britain had coal and iron ore close to each other. Agricultural improvements produced food surpluses, so fewer farmers were needed. A stable government protected property and patents. Colonies supplied raw materials and bought finished goods. Because capital was available, investors funded factories and canals.

## Effects on society
Cities grew quickly because workers moved to factory towns. Manchester's population rose from about 25,000 in 1750 to over 300,000 in 1850. Working conditions were harsh, with 12 to 16 hour days and child labour. However, real wages and life expectancy rose in the long run, and a new middle class emerged.

## Agricultural versus industrial society
Agricultural societies were rural, family based and tied to the seasons, whereas industrial societies were urban, wage based and tied to the clock. Agricultural output depended on land; industrial output depended on capital and machines. Social mobility was limited in the first and greater in the second.

## Lasting impact
The revolution created the modern factory system, mass transport and the idea of continuous economic growth. It also caused pollution and inequality, which later reforms tried to address.
